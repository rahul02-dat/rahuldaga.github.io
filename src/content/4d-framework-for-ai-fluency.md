Most people think AI fluency = writing better prompts. It doesn't.

While completing a course on Anthropic's Academy, I came across a document that put words to something I'd been noticing but couldn't quite articulate: the difference between someone who uses AI and someone who's genuinely fluent with it. It's called the 4D Framework for AI Fluency, developed through research collaboration between Prof. Rick Dakan (Ringling College of Art and Design) and Prof. Joseph Feller (University College Cork).

Four competencies. All interconnected.

**Delegation** — Before you open any AI tool, the real question isn't what to ask. It's whether to ask at all. Which parts of this work genuinely benefit from AI involvement? What requires your judgment, your relationships, your accountability? Poor delegation upstream means no prompt in the world will save the output downstream.

**Description** — This is what people reduce to "prompting." It's more than that. It's a communication skill. The precision with which you articulate your goal, your constraints, and the context behind the task determines the quality of what comes back.

**Discernment** — AI outputs are fluent, confident, and sometimes wrong. Discernment is reading every response the way a sharp editor reads a draft, not looking for what's right, actively hunting for what might be off. This is where domain expertise becomes irreplaceable. No tool replaces the human check.

**Diligence** — Whatever you put into the world that AI helped create, you own it. Fully. Not partially. Transparency about AI's role, accountability for the final output, thoughtfulness about which tools you're choosing and why, this is what separates professional use from careless use.

The framework isn't a checklist. It's a lens. And what makes it durable is that it applies regardless of which model you're using, which platform you're on, or what industry you work in. The tools will keep changing. These four competencies won't.

If you're integrating AI into serious work, these are the questions worth sitting with:

→ Are you thinking before you delegate, or defaulting to AI reflexively?
→ Can you describe your need precisely enough that the first output is actually close?
→ Are you reading outputs critically, or accepting what looks plausible?
→ Are you taking full ownership of what goes out with your name on it?
